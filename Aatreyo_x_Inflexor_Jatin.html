Where I Fit in Your Trinity

Your April framework doesn't look for one person who's a world-class scientist, engineer, and CEO at once — it looks for a Founding Trinity, distributed across two to four people. Here's an honest self-assessment against your own three roles, not a generic skills list.

Role 01

The Scientist — IP Anchor

"A deep domain expert who understands the first principles of the technology... ensures the company has a genuine proprietary moat."
Documented, not just theoretical
I hold 4 patent-pending IPs of my own, and completed an IP law certificate at the University of Pennsylvania — so "genuine proprietary moat" isn't an abstract phrase to me, it's something I've had to define and defend on paper. I still wouldn't claim the founder-level domain depth of someone building novel hardware or biology from scratch, but this is a real, documented seat in the Trinity, not just an LLM-adjacent claim.
Role 02

The Engineer — Executioner

"SET in India demands an 'Asset-Right' approach... robust, scalable, and cost-effective without burning Silicon Valley-scale capital."
Strongest fit, by a wide margin
This is where I start from strength, full stop. Every demo on this page is a shipped, working artifact — not a slide, not a mockup — built end to end by me, from the data model to the interaction you just clicked through. If you need someone who ships instead of just proposes, that's exactly what you're looking at right now.
Role 03

The Business Strategist — Navigator

"Translates complex science into a compelling, must-have ROI for customers... owns the journey from lab prototype to commercial product."
Growing fit
The investment-curiosity half of this role — reading a deal, understanding TAM, translating technical work for non-technical stakeholders — is exactly what these four interview rounds have been building toward. Not where I start strongest, but the direction I'm deliberately growing.
Most candidates would tell you they're all three. I'd rather tell you honestly where I land — because you wrote the founder red flags for a reason, and claiming a perfect fit across all three roles is exactly what "Tourist Founder" energy would look like on a resume.
