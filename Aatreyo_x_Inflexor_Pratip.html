Framing

Finding signal in the noise — applied to a hire, not a deal

You've said your engineering background helps you separate signal from noise when you're reading founders. I don't have a company to pitch you, so I built the signal instead: two tools the investment team could use starting month one, plus the written math on what they're worth.

Concept 01 — Copilot

Deal Triage Copilot

Takes a deal — deck, data room, or public record — and turns it into a clean, structured first read: Team, TAM, Technology scores plus a S.E.T. classification, using Inflexor's own public framework. It never decides anything. It prepares the read so the partner's first hour on a deal starts at judgment, not data entry.

Concept 02 — Agent

Portfolio Pulse Agent

Runs on its own schedule against every portfolio company, checks what's actually changed, and drafts a short update grounded in a real source — not a guess. No one has to remember to ask it. It just shows up with the note already written.

What I'd Actually Ask

Not warm-up questions — the ones I've genuinely been sitting with since researching this.

Question 01

You spent a decade in growth and buyout investing before moving to early-stage — what changed in how you evaluate a bet when you made that jump, and does any of that buyout-era discipline still show up in how you look at seed and Series A deals now?

Genuinely curious how someone recalibrates their own model across such different stages of the same craft.

Question 02

You've said your engineering training helps you find signal in the noise when evaluating founders. Does that same instinct apply to evaluating an early technical hire, or is that a completely different kind of signal to read?

Trying to understand if I'm being read the way you'd read a founder, or a different way entirely.

Question 03

You've talked about weighing societal and environmental impact alongside returns. BioPrime and Morphle both seem to fit that naturally — how do you think about it when a deal has strong returns potential but a weaker impact case, or vice versa?

A real tension worth understanding, not just a values checkbox.

Question 04

You're apparently the fund's go-to for recommendations in any city. Does that same instinct for what's genuinely good versus just well-marketed carry over into how you read a pitch?

Half-serious — but genuinely curious whether that instinct transfers.
